## Executive summary

Zen Rojas has a distinctive, trustworthy brand and clear functional positioning, but the storefront under-converts that goodwill at the moment of decision. The homepage carries strong testimonials, a veteran-owned family story, and benefit-led tiles (Sleep, Immune, Energy, Digestion), yet the product pages themselves show no reviews, ratings, or social proof — so the trust built on the homepage evaporates exactly where the buy decision happens. The single clearest leak is this proof gap on the PDP.

The second theme is weak basket economics. Teas are sold as single one-time SKUs ($8 Black, $13 Bodyguard) against a $50 free-shipping threshold, with a $10 Loose Leaf Sampler Set that exists but is buried as a product variant rather than merchandised as the obvious trial entry point. There is no subscribe-and-save on what are inherently reorderable consumables ("25 cups per bag," "sip daily for best results"), and the Ambassador Program is live but disconnected from the post-purchase moment — leaving repeat-purchase and referral revenue on the table.

Third, demand-generation assets are under-exploited: the blog already publishes recipes and press features but does not route that intent into use-case landing pages, and the captured product HTML contains no JSON-LD structured data, which weakens organic discovery. The experiments below span all five pillars and are sequenced so the PDP proof and basket fixes ship first, with acquisition and performance work following.
